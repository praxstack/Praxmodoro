Starting is the hard part.

Most tools are good at remembering. They hold the list and remind you again. But a reminder only repeats what the task is, so the task waits and grows heavier.

Praxmodoro works on the other side of that moment. It asks for one task and one step you could do in about two minutes, then keeps you company while you do it.

One calm loop.

Every session moves through the same five moments, so there’s nothing new to figure out each time you sit down.

Focus

A light that breathes slower than you.

The companion takes one long breath about every eleven seconds, with an exhale longer than the inhale. It never speeds up. Your task, your next step and the time stay in plain view, and nothing on the screen keeps score.

  • Park a stray thought without leaving the screen
  • A small capsule floats above other windows, and the menu bar shows the time
  • Hold the timer and your place is kept, exactly
{{> arc-3}}

Check in

A question, not a report card.

Now and then it asks how the step fits. There are four answers, and none of them is a failure: still fits, make it smaller, I drifted, I need a break. Each one only changes what happens next.

  • The timer holds while you answer
  • A detour is information. Return, re-plan or stop, all with equal weight
  • Keys 1 to 4 answer
{{> arc-4}}

Break

Rest that keeps your place.

Break ideas come only from what you told it, with a note that says why. End early whenever you like. When you come back, a re-entry card shows the exact next step, so you don’t rebuild the plan from memory.

  • Ending a break early is ordinary, never a penalty
  • Suggestions are editable and can be turned off
  • R returns to focus
{{> arc-5}}

Review

Today, written down gently.

A plain timeline of what happened: when you began, when you made a step smaller, when you rested. Notes stay tentative, because one afternoon is not a pattern.

  • No streaks, grades or productivity percentages
  • The record lives on your Mac

Then round again, or close the laptop. Both count.

The next session starts from the same quiet screen, with your last task ready if you want it.

Nothing here scores you.

No streaks to lose, no points, no red badges, no “you missed a day”. Drifting, pausing and stopping early are all ordinary.

  • Motion is your choice

    With Reduce Motion on, or Motion set to still, the companion stops completely and the time stays readable. The switch on this page does the same here.

  • The whole loop from the keyboard

    ⌘ ↩ begins, 1 to 4 answer a check-in, R returns from a break. Every control has a visible focus ring.

  • VoiceOver speaks plainly

    The field reads as a short status, like “Companion: breathing, 17 minutes remaining”. The decorative layers stay silent.

  • Contrast and transparency, respected

    Increase Contrast firms up text and edges. Reduce Transparency swaps the soft glass for solid backgrounds.

  • Few decisions

    Settings stay out of the start path. Sounds and notifications are optional, and they say plainly what they mean.

  • No medical claims

    ADHD-aware means designed around starting, drifting and coming back. It doesn’t diagnose or treat anything, and it never asks about a diagnosis.

It stays on your Mac.

There’s no account and no sign-in. Your tasks, steps, check-in answers and parked thoughts are stored on your Mac, and the app works without a network connection.

  • No analytics or tracking inside the app.
  • Nothing watches your other apps. Check-ins ask you; they never look over your shoulder.
  • AI only if you ask for it. The app has none today. A planned, opt-in helper would use Claude to suggest a first step when you’re stuck. It would stay off unless you turn it on.

And this website? It keeps only what you type into the waitlist form, plus a one-way hash of your IP address to stop spam. No cookies, no trackers, no ads. Read the privacy notice.

What does the beta cost?

Nothing. Praxmodoro is free while it’s in beta. Pricing after the beta isn’t decided yet. Beta testers will hear about it first, and you won’t be charged for anything you didn’t choose.

What do I need?

A Mac running macOS 26 or later. No account, no subscription, no other apps. The sign-up form asks whether your Mac has Apple silicon or an Intel chip, so testing can be planned around the Macs people actually use.

When can I get in?

Invites go out by email in small groups while the app is in private testing. There’s no fixed date yet and no queue position to watch. We’ll only email you about the beta.

Is Praxmodoro a medical app?

No. It’s a focus tool designed with ADHD and executive-function challenges in mind. It doesn’t diagnose, treat or monitor any condition, and it isn’t a substitute for care from a professional. You don’t need a diagnosis to use it, and it never asks about one.

Does it use AI?

Not today. Praxmodoro works completely without AI. One planned feature would use Claude, Anthropic’s AI model, to help you find a first step when a task feels too big to start.

The plan for it is simple: off by default, used only when you ask, and clear about exactly what it sends. Everything else stays on your Mac either way.

Is it a Pomodoro timer?

It can be. Classic 25 + 5 is one of the rhythms. The others are a five-minute gentle start that can grow into a full block, an open-ended flow block, and a rhythm that puts recovery first. You can set your own lengths too.

Will there be an iPhone, iPad or Windows version?

Not for now. Praxmodoro is a native Mac app, written in Swift for macOS, and the beta is about getting that right first.

Can I take my email off the waitlist?

Yes. Every beta email has an unsubscribe link, or write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} from the address you signed up with and it will be deleted.

Who makes it?

Prax, an independent developer in India. Praxmodoro is self-funded and small on purpose: one person, one app, and the people who test it.
